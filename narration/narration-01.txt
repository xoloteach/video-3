Imagine this.

You open Netflix.

And instead of watching something…

you spend twenty minutes deciding what to watch.

Then you close Netflix.

You open YouTube.

You scroll through hundreds of videos…

but still can't decide what to watch.

Then you check Instagram.

Then your messages.

Then maybe you open a shopping app.

You see dozens of products.

Different phones.

Different clothes.

Different laptops.

Different options.

And somehow…

having all these choices doesn't make you feel more free.

It makes you feel tired.

Because here's something strange about modern life.

We have more choices than humans have ever had.

But somehow…

we struggle to focus on anything.

We want to learn five skills.

Start three businesses.

Read ten books.

Go to the gym.

Learn a language.

Start creating content.

Make more money.

Travel.

Build a personal brand.

And somehow…

we expect ourselves to do all of it.

At the same time.

And when we can't…

we think something is wrong with us.

Maybe we're lazy.

Maybe we're undisciplined.

Maybe we just need more motivation.

But what if the problem isn't that you have too little discipline?

What if…

you simply have too many options?

There is a psychological phenomenon called choice overload.

The basic idea is simple.

When people are given too many choices…

making a decision can actually become harder.

You would think the opposite should be true.

More options should mean more freedom.

More freedom should mean better decisions.

But the human brain doesn't always work that way.
