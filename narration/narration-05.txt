And eventually…

you optimize yourself into inaction.

You don't need the perfect choice.

You need a choice you can commit to.

Think about successful people you admire.

Many of them didn't win because they discovered some secret opportunity that nobody else could see.

They often won because they stayed with something longer than most people were willing to.

They repeated.

They improved.

They became better.

They built experience.

They developed an advantage.

While everyone else was jumping from one thing to another…

they were still working.

This doesn't mean you should blindly stick with a bad decision forever.

Sometimes changing direction is exactly what you need.

But there is a difference between changing direction because you've learned something…

and changing direction because you're uncomfortable with commitment.

That's the part most people don't notice.

You start something.

The excitement is high.

Everything feels new.

Then the results don't come quickly.

So another opportunity starts looking attractive.

You switch.

Again.

And again.

And again.

Until years pass…

and you have experience starting things…

but very little experience finishing them.

The world doesn't reward everything you could have done.

It rewards what you actually do.

And that's why your ability to eliminate options can become a superpower.

Imagine waking up…

and already knowing what matters today.

You don't need to decide what to learn.

You already chose.

You don't need to decide what project to work on.

You already chose.
